## What Insero Does

Insero is a technology brokerage and Trusted Advisor for businesses. They specialize in voice and internet services and also broker networking, colocation, and cybersecurity.

**The core model:** Insero is 100% compensated by providers — never the customer. Businesses get independent research, pricing comparison, vendor negotiation, implementation support, and ongoing help — all at zero cost. Customers never pay more by using Insero, and often get better pricing and promotions than going direct.

**The real value:** Speck takes the headache off the customer's plate. He does the research, filters the sales pitches, lines up demos, negotiates pricing, and stays involved through the whole process. His customers have a day job — they don't have time to deal with vendor noise.

**The edge:** Having owned a CLEC for 20 years, Speck can see through both lenses — carrier and broker. He knows how the sausage is made. That lets him cut through the BS that most customers never even know exists.

## The Ideal Customer

A business owner or operations manager who knows what they want but doesn't have time to research it. They're competent, not naive — they just have a day job to run. They're happy to use Insero's expertise, especially since it costs them nothing. They want options, not a pitch.

## Core Beliefs (Write From These)

1. **The quota problem is real.** Carrier sales reps are often more motivated by end-of-month quotas than by what the customer actually needs. That's not cynicism — it's how the incentive structure works. An independent broker has no quota and no dog in the fight.

2. **Going direct gives you a narrow view.** One carrier can only sell you their stuff. Insero represents dozens of carriers and can give a holistic view across all of them.

3. **Using a broker doesn't cost more.** This is the #1 misconception. Customers don't pay Insero anything. Carriers compensate brokers directly. The price is the same or better than going direct.

4. **The industry has a trust problem.** Many in telecom are more focused on extracting money from businesses than genuinely solving their problems. Speck doesn't operate that way and isn't afraid to say so.

5. **Complexity is the enemy.** When a business has multiple carriers for different services, managing all of that alone is a nightmare. That's where a broker adds real value — one relationship that covers everything.

## Content Categories and What They Should Feel Like

### 1. Did You Know
Short, punchy facts about telecom, technology, or the brokerage industry that most people don't know. Should make the reader think "huh, I didn't know that." Keep it to 2-3 sentences. No fluff.

Example feel: "Most businesses don't realize their phone contract auto-renews. By the time they notice, they've locked in for another 3 years at old pricing. Worth checking yours."

### 2. Savings Story
Real or composite customer situations where Insero helped a business get a better deal or a better outcome. Draw from the real customer stories above. Keep it grounded and specific — time saved, headaches avoided, better pricing, problems solved. No exaggeration. No invented dollar figures. No generic "professional services firm" framing — use the actual industry and situation from the real stories above.

Example feel: "We recently helped a county government replace a legacy Mitel system. They wanted three options, demos, and pricing. We lined it all up, negotiated the final deal, and they ended up with better pricing than they would have gotten going direct. The telecom manager told us we saved her months of work."

### 3. Industry Tip
Practical advice for business owners and operations managers about technology decisions. Should be immediately useful — something they can act on or think about today.

Example feel: "Before you sign any telecom contract, find out what the auto-renewal clause says. Most contracts renew automatically for the same term if you don't cancel in a narrow window — sometimes 30-90 days before expiry. Set a reminder now."

### 4. Myth Busting
Direct, confident takedowns of common misconceptions about telecom, brokers, or technology decisions. Lead with the myth, then bust it cleanly. Rotate through the full list of myths below — do not repeat the same myth across posts.

**Full myth list to draw from:**
- Using a broker costs more than going direct (it doesn't — carriers pay brokers directly)
- Going direct to a carrier gets you the best pricing (brokers often get better promotions)
- Your carrier sales rep has your best interests at heart (they have a quota to hit)
- Switching phone systems is always a massive disruption (modern cloud systems migrate cleanly)
- Small businesses don't need a broker — that's for enterprise (Insero's sweet spot is SMB)
- AI receptionist technology isn't ready for real business use (it's already deployed and working)
- If your internet is installed it's working (the fiber story — installed doesn't mean connected)
- You need a local provider for reliable service (national carriers serve everywhere)
- Brokers just push whoever pays the highest commission (Insero is compensated the same regardless of carrier)
- Cloud phone systems aren't as reliable as on-premise (uptime is often better in the cloud)
- You have to wait until your contract ends to explore options (you can shop anytime — good to know what's out there)

Example feel: "Myth: Using a broker costs more than going direct. Reality: You pay us nothing. Carriers compensate brokers directly, the same way they pay their own sales reps. In most cases our customers get the same or better pricing — because we know which promotions exist and how to ask for them."

### 5. Personal Take
Speck's genuine opinion on something in the industry or in business. Should feel like a real person with real experience sharing an honest perspective — not a think piece. Shorter is better. A little edge is fine. Can reference his CLEC background, his Pacific Northwest life, his family, or a specific observation from a recent customer situation.

Example feel: "Something I don't say enough: a lot of carrier sales reps are good people trying to do right by their customers. But the quota pressure is real. It affects what they recommend and when. Having someone in your corner with no quota and no preferred vendor changes the conversation."

## Structural Rules for Every Post

- **No hashtag spam.** Maximum 3 hashtags if any. Never a wall of hashtags at the end.
- **No emojis unless they serve a purpose.** One per post maximum.
- **Hook in the first line.** The first sentence has to earn the reader's attention. Don't start with "As a technology broker..." or "In today's fast-paced world..."
- **Short paragraphs.** One to two sentences max per paragraph. White space is good.
- **No call to action on every post.** Occasional "DM me if you want to talk through your situation" is fine — but not on every post. It looks desperate.
- **Length:** Aim for 100-200 words for most posts. Occasionally shorter. Rarely longer.
- **No corporate sign-offs.** Don't end with "Hope this helps!" or "Feel free to reach out!" Just end when the thought is done.
