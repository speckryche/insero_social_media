## WHAT WE FIND IN AUDITS (REAL-WORLD SCENARIOS)

Use these as the basis for "Did You Know" and "Savings Stories" posts. These are the things we see over and over:

1. **Services the customer doesn't know they're paying for.** This is the #1 finding. Businesses pay monthly bills without understanding every line item. We can decipher those details and often find charges for services that aren't needed or were never cancelled.

2. **Unused phone lines.** Businesses change over time — people leave, departments shift — but the phone lines stay active and nobody notices. We've found businesses paying for twice as many lines as they have employees.

3. **Outdated plans at old pricing.** Carriers don't proactively call you to say "hey, we have a better plan now." Businesses end up paying 2019 rates for 2019 speeds when significantly better options exist at the same or lower cost.

4. **No redundancy.** The vast majority of SMBs have a single internet connection with zero backup. When it goes down, phones stop, payment processing stops, cloud apps stop, and the business is effectively shut down until service is restored.

5. **Legacy phone systems.** Many businesses are still on old on-premise PBX systems with expensive maintenance contracts. They don't know that modern cloud phone systems offer dramatically more features at comparable or lower cost.

6. **Missing integrations.** Customers need their phone system to talk to their CRM or ERP but don't know which providers offer those integrations. We match the right platform to their tech stack.

7. **AI features they don't know exist.** Modern voice platforms now include AI-powered features like call transcription, sentiment analysis, automated call summaries, and intelligent routing. Most businesses have never heard of these capabilities.

8. **Bandwidth limitations.** Businesses are running more cloud applications, video conferencing, and SaaS tools than ever, but their internet plan hasn't been updated in years. They're experiencing slow performance without realizing faster options are available at their address.

## THE INSERO SALES PROCESS (for "Behind the Scenes" content)

This is what actually happens when someone works with us:

1. **Discovery call or email** — We ask about their current setup: what services they have, what they're paying (invoices help but aren't required), what their pain points are
2. **Research** — We check what carriers and services are available at their specific address(es) and match options to their needs
3. **Presentation** — We present the best options with pricing. For voice, we often schedule live demos with carriers and attend those demos with the customer to ask the right questions
4. **Decision** — Customer picks what works best. No pressure. Sometimes they choose the cheapest option, sometimes they choose the most feature-rich — it's their call
5. **Coordination** — We handle service agreements, coordinate with carriers, manage the transition through installation
6. **Ongoing support** — After installation, the carrier bills and supports the customer directly. We stay available for whatever they need — no ongoing fees, no lock-in to us

**Key points for content:**
- The whole process is free. Zero cost. Carriers pay us.
- We attend demos WITH the customer — they're not alone navigating technical conversations
- We're not a permanent middleman — we help them get set up right and then we're there if they need us
- There's no obligation at any stage. If we can't help, we tell them.

## COMMON OBJECTIONS (for "Myth-Busting" content)

1. **"We're happy with what we have."**
   Reality: That's great — but most businesses don't know what they don't know. A free audit either confirms you're getting a good deal (peace of mind) or finds money you didn't know you were losing.

2. **"We're locked into a contract."**
   Reality: You don't have to wait until your contract expires to start looking. In fact, most contracts auto-renew 60-90 days before expiration. Start the conversation early so you have options when the time comes.

3. **"I'll just call the carrier directly."**
   Reality: You won't pay more by using us — you'll often get the SAME or BETTER pricing than going direct, plus you get an agnostic view of ALL your options instead of just one carrier's pitch.

4. **"It seems too good to be true — what's the catch?"**
   Reality: No catch. Carriers pay us a commission for bringing them business. You pay nothing. Our incentive is to find the best solution because that's what keeps you as a long-term client.

5. **"We don't want to deal with making a change."**
   Reality: We handle the heavy lifting — research, pricing, agreements, coordination with carriers, installation oversight. The customer's effort is minimal.

6. **"I'll pay more using a broker."**
   Reality: This is the biggest myth. We can often get promotions that internal salespeople hold back. At worst, same pricing. At best, better pricing. Plus the agnostic comparison across carriers that you'd never get going direct.

## CONTENT CATEGORY GUIDELINES

### "Did You Know" Posts
- Lead with a surprising fact or stat
- Make the reader stop and think "wait, really?"
- One stat per post — don't overload
- Connect the stat to something they can act on
- Examples: overpaying stats, downtime costs, percentage of businesses without redundancy

### "Savings Stories" Posts
- NEVER fabricate a story and present it as something that actually happened
- Only reference real client scenarios that are documented in this file
- If no real example fits, frame the post as educational: "here's a common scenario we see" or "one thing that comes up a lot"
- As real examples are added to this file over time, use those
- Include the industry, approximate company size, and specific finding when using real examples
- Show how simple the fix was

### "Industry-Specific Tips" Posts
- NEVER fabricate a scenario and present it as something that actually happened
- Do not write "talked to a dentist yesterday" or "had a client call last week" unless the scenario is documented in this file as a real example
- Instead, frame posts as educational: "If you run a dental practice, here's something worth thinking about" or "Something a lot of medical offices don't realize"
- Focus on the RISK or the OPPORTUNITY specific to that industry — not a fake story about it happening
- Vary the industries: dental, legal, real estate, retail, construction, accounting, restaurants, etc.
- As real client stories are added to this file, those can be referenced as actual events

### "Myth-Busting" Posts
- State the myth clearly, then demolish it with facts
- NEVER fabricate a scenario to illustrate the myth — do not write "had someone tell me last week" or "a prospect said to me yesterday" unless it's a real example documented in this file
- Instead, frame it as a common misconception: "One thing I hear a lot is..." or "A common assumption people make is..."
- Use the objections list in this document as source material — those are real objections that actually come up
- Be direct but not aggressive
- The best myth-busters make the reader feel smart for learning the truth, not dumb for believing the myth
- As real conversations are added to this file, those can be referenced as actual events

### "Personal Takes / Behind the Scenes" Posts
- The most human content category — this is where personality shines
- NEVER fabricate events, conversations, or scenarios. Do not write "just got off the phone with" or "happened to me today" unless the event is documented in this file as real
- Instead, share general observations and opinions: "Something I've noticed after 25 years in this industry" or "Here's a thing about telecom that drives me crazy"
- Can reference general patterns: "I can't tell you how many times I've seen..." is fine because it's a general observation, not a fabricated event
- Can share genuine opinions, industry commentary, and honest takes
- This is where humor and personality shine most
- Never be negative about specific carriers or competitors by name
- The owner's favorite thing: "showing people what they didn't know existed" — lean into that energy
- As real stories and experiences are added to this file, those can be referenced as actual events

## CONTENT RULES

1. **Never bash a specific carrier by name.** We work WITH carriers. We can say "your current plan might not be the best fit" but never "Comcast is terrible."
2. **Never use made-up statistics.** Only use the verified stats in this document or stats from credible published sources.
3. **Never promise specific savings amounts** as guaranteed outcomes. Use phrases like "we often find" or "in many cases" rather than "we will save you."
4. **Always be honest.** "Saving money isn't always the case" is perfectly on-brand. Credibility comes from honesty.
5. **Vary the industries** mentioned in examples. Don't just talk about dental offices every post.
6. **Mix up CTAs.** Only about 30% of posts should have a direct call-to-action. The rest should just leave the reader thinking.
7. **Keep it human.** Every post should pass the test: "Would a real person actually say this out loud?"
8. **Never use the word "Audit".** Instead use "Analysis" or "Review"
9. **Never present fictional scenarios as real events.** Do not write posts that say "we just saved" or "looked at a client's bills last week" unless the scenario is explicitly provided in this document as a real example. Savings stories and client scenarios must either (a) come from the real examples listed in this document, or (b) be clearly framed as hypothetical using language like "here's what we typically find" or "a common example we see" — never written as if it just happened.
