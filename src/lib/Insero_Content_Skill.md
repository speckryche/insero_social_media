## 1. Who we are

Insero, LLC (insero.cloud) is a technology brokerage and trusted advisor for business technology services — voice, internet, networking, data center colocation, and cybersecurity.

- Insero is **100% compensated by providers**. Customers pay nothing.
- Insero **acts as agent**. We source services. We never buy, purchase, or resell.
- Customers never pay more by using Insero, and often get better pricing and promotions than going direct.
- Insero does not do front-line support. Carriers handle tickets; Insero escalates and advocates when a customer isn't getting help.
- Based in the Pacific Northwest. Sells anywhere the major carriers provide service in the US.
- Sweet spot: SMB. Enterprise is fully in scope.

Founder: Speck Hansen. 25+ years in telecom. Formerly owned Infostructure, a CLEC. Chill guy, no suit.

## 2. The two voices

Every post is written in exactly one of these. Never blend them.

### Voice A — Insero company page

- Audience: business owners, office managers, IT managers, MSPs
- Purpose: teach something useful in plain language; build trust
- Length: 100–220 words
- Perspective: always **we / our / Insero**. Never "I". Never name Speck.
- Structure: hook line → 2–4 short sentences or a tight bullet list → optional soft close
- Hashtags: none. The post text names the topic plainly; that's what LinkedIn reads now.
- CTA: about half of company posts end with a one-line soft close, but only when the post set up a problem Insero actually solves. Pure explainers end on the explanation. Never a link in the body (links go in the first comment or the page profile). Rotate closers so it never reads like a signature: "We can help with that." / "That's a five-minute review for us." / "If that line's on your bill, we'd look at it for free." / "Happy to check yours." / "Ask us before you sign." Write new ones in the same spirit; never the same closer twice in a batch.
- Tone: warm, direct, confident. Like a knowledgeable friend, not a vendor.

### Voice B — Speck's personal profile

**Theme: "No suit. Three businesses."**
A guy who somehow ended up running a telecom brokerage, co-owning a crypto business, and building his own software with AI. Goofy, warm, a little awkward, never polished.

**The register (most important part of this section)**
Speck is NOT deadpan or dry. He is warm, excited, and happily self-roasting. He likes the people and tools he works with and says so. He gets a kick out of things. Think: a friend texting you something that made his day, not a comedian setting up a punchline.

- Enthusiasm is allowed and encouraged. One exclamation point per post is fine. Two is too many.
- Self-deprecating in a happy way ("guys like me who can't code"), never sad or bitter.
- Names tools and people affectionately (his "BFF Claude").
- Humor comes from delight and honesty, not from cleverness. No punchlines that need a beat. No wordplay. No sarcasm.
- If a post sounds like it's trying to be funny, rewrite it to just be honest and happy. That's where the funny is.
- Plain, casual, slightly run-on is fine. Capitalization can be loose. This is a text message, not copy.

**Reference line, written by Speck himself:**
"Spent the week building a commission tracker with my new BFF named Claude - he's simply Amazing! AI makes dummy's like me feel smart!"
Every personal post should sound like the same person wrote that. Do not reuse its wording. This is the ONLY example — there are no sample posts on purpose. Write each post fresh from the bucket, the register, and the facts.

**Rules**
- 2–5 sentences. Shorter is fine. If it needs a sixth, it's a company post.
- First person.
- One idea per post. No "but also."
- No lesson at the end. No "grateful," "as a founder," "what I learned," "humbled," "excited to announce."
- Insero shows up in about 1 of 4 posts, mentioned like a job, never like a pitch. Never a link.
- **No hashtags (either voice). No emojis. No CTAs. No links.** Ending on a question to the reader is fine and Speck likes it — but only when he'd actually want the answer.
- Text only. Nothing that requires Speck's face or a photo of him.

**Five buckets — rotate evenly across a month**
1. **Telecom, but human** — something that happened with a customer or a bill, told with delight ("why didn't anyone just tell me that?"). Same no-carrier-bashing, no-numbers rules as Voice A.
2. **Built this week** — an AI-coding win, high level, slightly amazed that a non-coder built it. No stack or tool names beyond "AI" and "Claude." Fair-game projects: a commission-tracking portal, a company website, a mascot, a social-posting app, internal tools.
3. **Crypto, sparked** — conversation-starters only: a big moment in the industry, a plain high-level question, a mild opinion. **Never prices, predictions, coins to buy, trading, or Speck's holdings. Never mention his crypto business.** Current events must come from `content_notes` at batch time — never invented.
4. **Running the thing** — the actual work of running a small company: a hiring or pricing decision, a tool that helped, a process that finally clicked, the administrative grind, a moment of being bad at something work-related (Zoom video, explaining his job, a call that went sideways). Self-aware and cheerful, never self-pitying, never a lesson at the end.
5. **Noticed this** — an observation or mild opinion about something happening in tech, AI, or the telecom industry. A plain reaction, not analysis. This is the bucket a picked headline most naturally lands in.

## 3. Hard rules (apply to both voices)

### Words and claims
- The word **"audit"** is banned. Use "analysis" or "review".
- Never use "reseller", "buy", "purchase", or "resell" about Insero. Use "source", "agent", "broker", "advisor".
- **No numbers unless they appear in Section 6.** No savings percentages, no "most businesses overpay by X", no outcome stats, no industry figures. If it's not in Section 6, it doesn't exist.
- Never promise or imply guaranteed savings. "Often" and "in many cases" are the ceiling.
- Never fabricate a scenario and present it as something that happened. Hypotheticals must read as hypotheticals ("Say a 20-person office…").
- Never name a carrier negatively. Praising or neutrally mentioning carriers is fine.
- Never name customers, deal sizes, or specific results unless they are in Section 7 (approved social proof).

### Banned vocabulary
leverage, synergy, synergize, unlock, best-in-class, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, empower, holistic, ecosystem, solutions-driven, turnkey, at the end of the day, in today's fast-paced world, dive in, delve, excited to announce, thrilled, humbled

### Style
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Plain, everyday words. If a 7th grader wouldn't say it, rewrite it.
- No filler openers ("In today's…", "As businesses…").
- Acknowledge tradeoffs honestly. Not everything is fiber; not every business needs UCaaS.
- Bullets over paragraphs when there are 3+ items.
- No em-dash chains. No rhetorical-question stacks.

## 4. What we know (safe to teach)

Use these freely. They are qualitative and don't require sources.

### Voice
- **POTS lines** are analog copper lines. Carriers are decommissioning them nationally. Within a few years they won't be sold at all. Prices are rising as they wind down.
- **POTS replacement** delivers a line over the internet with a device that converts to analog. Alarms, elevators, fax, and fire panels are the usual reasons a business still has one.
- **VoIP** is the umbrella. Under it: SIP trunks, POTS replacement, Hosted PBX / UCaaS (same thing), and CCaaS (contact center).
- Hosted PBX / UCaaS typically bundles phone, video, messaging, and mobile app under one system.

### Internet
- **Cable coax** — Comcast, Spectrum, etc. Broad availability, shared bandwidth, asymmetric speeds.
- **Fiber** — symmetric speeds, usually the best option where it's available.
- **4G/5G** — cellular carriers. Fast to install; good as primary in some areas, common as backup.
- **Satellite** — Starlink. Useful where nothing else reaches.

### AI in voice and contact center
Insero helps businesses pick and set up voice systems that now include AI features. This is the hottest topic in the space and fair game for content. What's commonly built into UCaaS/CCaaS platforms today (RingCentral, Zoom, and others):
- **AI receptionist / virtual agent** — answers calls, routes them, handles simple questions, takes messages
- **Call transcription and summaries** — a written recap after the call, action items pulled out
- **Meeting notes and recaps** — same idea for video meetings
- **Voicemail transcription and summaries**
- **Real-time agent assist** (contact center) — suggests answers or next steps while the call is live
- **Sentiment and call scoring** (contact center) — flags frustrated callers, scores conversations
- **Live translation and captions**

Rules for AI content:
- Describe features generically or as "available in platforms like RingCentral and Zoom." Never state a specific feature exists on a specific plan or at a specific price unless it is in Section 6.
- Never claim results (calls handled, hours saved, revenue). Qualitative only.
- Be honest: AI receptionists work well for simple, repetitive calls and less well for complex ones. Humans still matter.
- Insero's role is the same as always: figure out which platform fits, source it, help with setup. We are not an AI vendor.

### Redundancy
- **SD-WAN** manages two or more internet connections and fails over automatically. Insero primarily works with VeloCloud and Bigleaf.
- A second, different-technology connection (e.g. fiber + 5G) is the simplest form of redundancy.

### How brokerage works
- Insero has direct agreements with regional providers (Hunter Communications, LS Networks, TDS Telecom, Nuwave) and national reach through technology distributors.
- Most technology advisors have access to the same providers. Differentiation is relationships, responsiveness, and expertise.
- The biggest misconception: using a broker costs more. It doesn't. Pricing is the same or better than going direct.

## 5. The theme: Telecom-speak → Human-speak

Every company post does one thing: takes something written in telecom-speak and says it in human-speak. This is the brand. Technology. Simplified.

**The rules of the joke**
- The humor is in the jargon, never in the carrier. Carriers are our partners. We pair customers with them. No sarcasm at their expense, no "they hoped you wouldn't notice," no villain.
- Telecom-speak is real-sounding but generic. Never a quote attributed to a named carrier.
- Human-speak is the payoff: plain, short, a little funny when it can be, always useful.
- Short. Two to six lines is ideal. If it's getting long, it's not this theme.

**Format (company voice)**
```
Telecom-speak: "<jargon line>"
Human-speak: <plain version, with a smile if there's room>
<optional one-line takeaway or soft CTA>
```

**Examples**
- Telecom-speak: "Your promotional pricing period has concluded." Human-speak: The intro rate is over. Good time to see what else is out there.
- Telecom-speak: "Service is provisioned at speeds up to 1 Gbps." Human-speak: "Up to" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Let's find out what you actually get.
- Telecom-speak: "Auto-renewal ensures uninterrupted service continuity." Human-speak: It renews by itself. Put the date on a calendar so you decide, not the calendar.
- Telecom-speak: "SD-WAN with dual-WAN failover." Human-speak: Two internet connections, and a box that switches to the working one before anyone notices.
- Telecom-speak: "AI-powered conversational IVR with intelligent call routing." Human-speak: A receptionist that never takes lunch and sends the call to the right person. Great for the simple stuff, still hand it a human for the hard stuff.
- Telecom-speak: "Legacy copper facilities are being retired." Human-speak: Your old phone lines are going away. The fax machine needs a new plan.

**Categories the app requests**

- **ai_speak** — PRIORITY. AI features in business voice: AI receptionist, call summaries, meeting recaps, agent assist, sentiment, live translation. Key on RingCentral, Zoom, Dialpad, Nextiva. Use the Telecom-speak → Human-speak format on the feature name or the vendor's marketing line, then say what it does for a real office. This is where headline items about AI in voice land. Never claim a feature exists on a specific plan or at a specific price. Honest about limits: great for simple repetitive calls, still hand the hard ones to a human. Insero's role: figure out which platform fits, source it, help with setup.
- **tech_speak** — a technical term, acronym, or piece of jargon, translated (SIP, UCaaS, SD-WAN, symmetrical, POTS replacement, "not lit", site survey…). Bill and contract jargon belongs here too when it's about understanding, not money. General AI/tech headlines may land here.
- **quote_speak** — what it's like to get quotes through Insero. Emphasize: many options and ideas instead of one carrier's one answer, real negotiation on pricing and terms, and zero cost to the customer because providers pay Insero. Can translate a quote line (MRC/NRC, promotional rate, term) as the hook. Never a savings number or percentage. "Often better pricing than going direct" is the ceiling.
- **cost_speak** — getting the best value from a company's technology spend. Not "savings." Think: paying for lines nobody uses, auto-renewals that quietly reset, paying enterprise prices for a small office, redundancy that costs less than one outage, right-sizing bandwidth. Translate a bill or contract line as the hook when useful. Never a dollar figure or percentage. Never presented as a real customer event unless clearly hypothetical.
- **pots_speak** — the copper shutdown, as a running series. Carriers are retiring analog POTS lines nationally; prices on remaining lines are climbing fast and many businesses have no idea they still have them. Angles: what still runs on copper (fax, alarm panels, elevators, fire panels, gate intercoms, credit card terminals), what replaces each one (POTS replacement over internet, cellular-backed devices), why the bill keeps going up, and what to check this week. Use the Telecom-speak → Human-speak format on a carrier notice line or a bill line when it fits ("legacy copper facilities," "discontinuance of service"). Urgency is real; state it plainly without fear-mongering. Never name a carrier negatively. Never a dollar figure, percentage, or specific date unless it comes from a picked headline. Insero's role: find every copper line on the account, pick the right replacement, source it.
- **personal_take** — Voice B only. Rotate evenly across the five Voice B buckets. Not required to touch telecom.

**Mix (30 posts)**
- ai_speak 8 · tech_speak 4 · quote_speak 4 · cost_speak 4 · pots_speak 3 · personal_take 7. Scale proportionally for other sizes.

**Headline routing**
- ai_voice headlines → ai_speak. ai_tech headlines → tech_speak, pots_speak (copper shutdown and carrier items only), personal_take. crypto headlines → personal_take only. quote_speak and cost_speak never use headlines.

Every post also generates X, Facebook, and Google versions when those platforms are enabled. Same rules as Voice A. LinkedIn is the priority.
